# Manuscript outline — revised after the frozen and adaptive analyses

## Proposed title

*Chronological-age transcriptomic directions are not portable scalar readouts of partial reprogramming*

## One-sentence abstract claim placeholder

Across temporally separated public partial-reprogramming studies, transcriptomic directions that
correctly oriented young and old controls disagreed on intervention direction and did not support a
universal scalar rejuvenation readout.

This is a benchmark conclusion, not evidence that any source intervention lacks functional,
epigenetic, or organismal effects. It must not imply that the models measured biological age,
causality, efficacy, or safety.

2. **Primary frozen framework.** Reference-age performance, discovery-stage axis separation, and the

3. **Cross-model benchmark.** Corrected outer-CV performance for five evaluable age-direction models,

4. **Intervention disagreement.** Complete method-by-contrast matrix, pairwise sign agreement, and

5. **Gene-level non-convergence.** Pairwise family effect correlations and leave-one-family-out
   consensus correlations on common mapped genes.
6. **Implications for rejuvenation claims.** A qualification framework separating age association,
   intervention response, identity/pluripotency change, and functional validation.
